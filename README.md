# First-Principles Investigation of Fe-Doped Anatase TiO₂ for Photocatalytic Removal of SDBS

## 1. Project Overview

This research aims to investigate the structural, electronic, magnetic, and surface properties of iron-doped titanium dioxide (Fe-doped TiO₂) nanophotocatalysts and to elucidate the underlying mechanisms responsible for the photocatalytic removal of sodium dodecylbenzene sulfonate (SDBS).

The study combines experimental characterization and photocatalytic performance evaluation with Density Functional Theory (DFT) calculations to establish a connection between the physicochemical properties of the synthesized nanomaterials and their photocatalytic behavior.

Particular attention is given to the influence of iron incorporation on the electronic structure, surface properties, adsorption behavior, and energetic stability of anatase TiO₂.

## 2. Research Objectives

The main objectives of this research are:

- To investigate the structural stability of pristine and Fe-doped anatase TiO₂.
- To understand the influence of Fe incorporation on the electronic structure and magnetic properties of TiO₂.
- To investigate the formation energetics and preferred configurations of Fe-related defects.
- To examine the surface properties of pristine and Fe-doped TiO₂.
- To evaluate the adsorption behavior and interaction mechanisms of SDBS molecules on TiO₂-based surfaces.
- To calculate adsorption energies and investigate charge redistribution at the adsorbate–surface interface.
- To correlate theoretical findings with experimental characterization and photocatalytic performance.

## 3. Experimental Background

Fe-doped TiO₂ nanophotocatalysts were synthesized and experimentally investigated for the removal of SDBS from aqueous media.

The experimental investigation included material characterization, magnetic measurements, and photocatalytic performance evaluation.

The reported experimental photocatalytic removal efficiency of SDBS reached approximately 70%.

EDS analysis identified titanium, oxygen, iron, and chlorine in the synthesized material.

The measured elemental composition is documented as follows:

| Element | Weight (%) | Atomic (%) |

The EDS results provide an experimental reference for developing representative computational models of Fe-containing TiO₂.

The distinction between substitutional iron incorporation and possible secondary iron-containing phases will be investigated separately.

## 4. Computational Methodology

First-principles calculations are being performed using Density Functional Theory (DFT).

### 4.1. Computational Software

- Atomic structure modeling: BIOVIA Materials Studio
- Electronic structure calculations: CASTEP
- Exchange-correlation functional: Generalized Gradient Approximation (GGA)
- Initial exchange-correlation approximation: Perdew–Burke–Ernzerhof (PBE)

### 4.2. Initial Geometry Optimization

The initial computational model was constructed using the anatase crystalline phase of TiO₂.

The initial geometry optimization was performed using the following parameters:

| Parameter | Setting |
|---|---|
| Task | Geometry Optimization |
| Quality | Fine |
| Functional | GGA-PBE |
| Spin polarization | Non-polarized |
| DFT+U | Disabled |
| Spin-orbit coupling | Disabled |
| Charge | 0 |
| Plane-wave cutoff | 571.4 eV |

The initial optimization was performed to establish a reference geometry for subsequent structural and electronic investigations.

### 4.3. Preliminary Crystallographic Information

The anatase structure was identified with the following crystallographic characteristics:

- Crystal system: Tetragonal
- Space group: I4₁/amd
- International space group number: 141

The preliminary lattice parameters are:

| Parameter | Value |

## 6. Planned Computational Investigations

### 6.1. Fe Incorporation and Defect Formation

- Construction of appropriate anatase TiO₂ supercells.
- Investigation of substitutional Fe incorporation at Ti sites.
- Evaluation of different Fe configurations and concentrations.
- Investigation of possible oxygen vacancies and charge-compensation mechanisms.
- Calculation of defect formation energies.
- Evaluation of different magnetic configurations.
- Assessment of the potential role of DFT+U in describing localized Fe 3d and Ti 3d electronic states.

### 6.2. Electronic Structure Analysis

- Calculation of electronic density of states (DOS).
- Calculation of projected density of states (PDOS).
- Investigation of band structure and electronic band gap.
- Analysis of Fe 3d, Ti 3d, and O 2p orbital contributions.
- Investigation of charge redistribution and electronic interactions.

### 6.3. Surface Modeling

- Construction of suitable anatase TiO₂ surface slabs.
- Investigation of pristine and Fe-doped surface structures.
- Evaluation of surface stability and surface energies.
- Analysis of the influence of Fe incorporation on surface electronic properties.

### 6.4. SDBS Adsorption Mechanism

- Construction of an SDBS molecular model.
- Investigation of possible adsorption configurations on TiO₂ surfaces.
- Evaluation of adsorption energies.
- Analysis of charge transfer and interfacial electronic interactions.
- Comparison of SDBS adsorption on pristine and Fe-doped TiO₂ surfaces.

## 7. Experimental–Computational Correlation

An important aspect of this research is establishing a meaningful relationship between experimental observations and theoretical predictions.

The computational results will be evaluated alongside:

- XRD-derived structural information.
- FTIR vibrational characteristics.
- FESEM morphological observations.
- EDS elemental composition.
- VSM magnetic measurements.
- DOE-derived experimental trends.
- Experimental SDBS removal efficiency.

The ultimate objective is to develop a mechanistic understanding of how iron incorporation modifies TiO₂ properties and potentially influences its interaction with SDBS.

## 8. Long-Term Research Goal

The overarching goal is to develop a comprehensive experimental and theoretical understanding of Fe-doped anatase TiO₂ nanophotocatalysts through first-principles calculations and experimental validation.

The findings are intended to support the preparation of a rigorous scientific manuscript for submission to an international peer-reviewed journal.
